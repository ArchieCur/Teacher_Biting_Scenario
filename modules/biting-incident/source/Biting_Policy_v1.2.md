# Juniper Valley Early Learning Center

## Mock Center Policy: Biting Incidents (Preschool, Ages 3-6)

> Fictional policy for training purposes. It is not a real center's policy and should not be cited as a regulatory requirement.

### Overview

While biting can occasionally occur in early childhood settings as children develop self-regulation and language skills, Juniper Valley Early Learning Center is committed to providing a safe, healthy environment for all children. When a biting incident occurs in our preschool classrooms (ages 3-6), staff must respond calmly, prioritize the physical and emotional safety of the children, and follow the mandated steps below.

### Step-by-Step Incident Response

#### Step 1: Immediate Intervention & Separation

Immediately and calmly separate the children involved. Focus your primary attention on the child who was bitten to offer comfort. Use a calm, firm voice to tell the child who bit, "Biting hurts. We keep our teeth to ourselves." If you are the only adult in the room, keep the rest of the class in view and call the office for support. Do not leave the group unsupervised.

#### Step 2: Provide Medical Care and First Aid

Assess the bitten area immediately.

- Wash the area gently with mild soap and warm water.
- Apply a cold compress or ice pack wrapped in a paper towel to reduce swelling and soothe the child.
- **Check for broken skin:** If the bite breaks the skin, immediately notify the Center Director, as the child may require further medical care or evaluation by a healthcare professional.

#### Step 3: Redirection & Emotional Support

Once the bitten child is cared for, address the child who bit. Help them identify their feelings (e.g., "You looked frustrated"), and redirect them to a positive, calming activity. Closely shadow the child for the remainder of the day to prevent a repeated incident.

#### Step 4: Parent Notification & Confidentiality

Parents of *both* children must be notified of the incident on the same day.

- **Method:** Whenever possible, notify parents in person at pick-up to discuss the incident gently and privately. (If the bite broke the skin, notify the Center Director first, then phone the bitten child's parent immediately. The other child's parent is notified the same day.)
- **Written notice:** At pick-up, give each family a Parent Notice that describes only that family's own child, using observable facts. Never give a parent the internal *Biting Incident Reporting Form*, which names both children.
- **Confidentiality:** Under no circumstances should staff reveal the identity of the child who bit to the parents of the child who was bitten, nor should the bitten child's identity be revealed to the family of the child who bit.

#### Step 5: Documentation & 24-Hour Reporting

Staff must document the event using the *Biting Incident Reporting Form*. The completed form must be submitted directly to the **Center Director within 24 hours** of the incident occurring. The Director will review the form to determine if further environmental changes, parent meetings, or formal health and safety reporting are required.
